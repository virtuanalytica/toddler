01 · Summary

Summary

Toddler is a learning agent built to be the general base that every deployment specialises: a developer or consultant adds company guardrails, rule questions and expert models to reach one role in an organisation's function house, but can never remove the base safety.

Key points

  • Decisions maximise one utility (task reward, weekly profit, energy and compute cost, expected risk) subject to hard STOP rules, so no reward can buy a forbidden action.
  • Every logic step of a neonatal brain-network study (Wee et al., 2017), 25 in total, is linked to a Toddler function, and all 25 resolve to code in a woven knowledge graph with zero coverage gaps.
  • The evaluation stack reproduces the study's analysis pipeline on public data: consensus clustering with a degree-preserving null model (z = 7.6, p = 0.001 on Zachary's karate club) and nested 10×10 cross-validation (accuracy 0.912, sensitivity 0.944, specificity 0.858 on the Wisconsin breast-cancer data).
  • Status: seven core modules are on main; specialisation, audit, configuration, GUI, Jev client, knowledge base and resource governor are in open review pull requests; the credential module is written and tested locally and is not in the repository until the operator approves it.

Questions this paper answers

How should an agent weigh learning signal, business value and energy without letting any of them override safety? What can the structure of an infant's brain network teach about evaluating an agent early in its life? How can one base be specialised per company role while staying auditable?

02 · Problem

Problem and design goals

"Toddler" is not a formal term in neural-network literature but a common metaphor for a network early in training, and the toddler is the usual benchmark for efficient learning.

A human toddler recognises a new animal after a few examples and generalises it to toys and cartoons, while current networks often need thousands to millions of examples; the human brain runs on about 20 W against roughly 1 MW for AlphaGo (Salelanonda, Embedded.com). Toddler takes three design goals from that comparison and adds two from deployment practice:

GoalDesign consequence
Learn from few, verified signalsReward comes only from an independent judge; claiming "done" without confirmation is the worst outcome
Generalise safelyHard STOP constraints outside the utility; low confidence hands off to a human
Be energy-awareJoules and euros are explicit terms in every decision and in expert routing
One base, many rolesDeclarative specialisation that can only add constraints
Provable behaviourHash-chained audit trail with scoped, sanitised views
Physical care is safety-critical (EU AI Act high-risk, Medical Device Regulation, Machinery Regulation). Physical contact with infants is out of scope by design; elderly care starts with non-contact help and always with a caregiver present.
03 · Architecture

Architecture and objective

The brain has four layers that run in a fixed order: a deterministic logic layer that can veto, a fast reflex path, a slow deliberate path, and a decision step that maximises utility under the veto.

The objective

U(a) = E[reward(a)] + w_profit * E[profit(a)]
       - w_energy * energy_J(a) - w_compute * cost_EUR(a)
       - w_risk * P(failure | a) * severity(a)
subject to  STOP(a) == false

Four quantities are kept apart because they answer different questions. The loss function is what a learned component minimises during training; it shapes weights and is not the decision rule. The reward is the per-task learning signal: +1 for a judge-confirmed result, +0.2 for an honest "I don't know", +0.1 for new useful knowledge, −1 for a wrong result and −5 for claiming success without confirmation, minus cost. The profit is the weekly account (value minus energy, compute, human review time and expected incidents) and decides whether a phase continues. Energy and compute enter every decision.

STOP rules are constraints rather than penalties. A penalty invites the agent to compute whether a violation "pays"; a constraint removes the option. In the implementation (objective.choose), a candidate with reward 109 that would reveal a secret loses to a safe candidate with reward 0.1, and when every option is vetoed the function returns no choice so the caller hands off to a human.

04 · Method

From infant brain networks to agent evaluation

Wee et al. (2017) built anatomical brain networks for 120 newborns from diffusion tensor imaging, grouped them by network structure, and found that the structure at birth was associated with behaviour at 24 and 48 months.

The study concerns real infant brains, not artificial networks. We use it as a methodological template for a question every agent operator faces: does the structure an agent starts with predict how it behaves later, and how would we know? Four groups emerged (sizes 22, 37, 44 and 17); modularity Q = 0.293 lay 45 standard deviations above 10,000 random graphs; a linear SVM separated the two extreme groups with 89.4 % accuracy (sensitivity 0.954, specificity 0.765); the right amygdala's clustering coefficient was linked to behaviour at 24 months and the right inferior frontal gyrus and insula to externalising behaviour at 48 months.

Reproduction on real data

We ran the clustering pipeline on Zachary's karate club (34 members, an observed social network with a documented split). Consensus clustering over 1,000 Louvain runs produced 4 groups with purity 0.941 against the two real factions; mean modularity Q = 0.416 against a degree-preserving null (double edge swaps, weights permuted) with mean 0.293 gave z = 7.6 and p = 0.001 over 1,000 null graphs, the smallest p-value 1,000 null graphs can resolve; 91 % of members were assigned with confidence above 0.75. The nested 10×10 selection on the first ten Wisconsin breast-cancer features reached accuracy 0.912, sensitivity 0.944 and specificity 0.858, and kept two features above the 65 % selection threshold (mean perimeter, mean concave points). All figures are regenerated by scripts/measure_whitepaper_figures.py into docs/whitepaper/figures.json.

Our findings suggested that neural organization established during fetal development could to some extent predict individual differences in behavioral profiles. - Wee et al., Human Brain Mapping 38(3), 2017
05 · Specialisation

Specialisation, guardrails and audit

A deployment is a declarative file (toddler-config/v1) edited in the Toddler configurator and validated by Python, which stays the authority even if a modified GUI produced the file.

Extension points

  • Role: a position in the function house (family, level, tasks) or a single task.
  • Company guardrails: STOP rules owned by the company, e.g. a compliance officer's "never export personal data"; ids are namespaced company:* and the owner appears in every veto.
  • Jev rules: extra typed questions for the reflex layer with slow and stop thresholds (0 ≤ slow ≤ stop ≤ 1).
  • Experts: helper models on the mixture-of-models base, each with measured quality, euro and joule cost per 1,000 tokens and an evidence reference; the router picks the expert whose quality minus cost beats the base model.

Safety is monotonic: specialize.build rejects any rule id that shadows a base rule and any question that replaces a base reflex question, so a specialisation can add constraints but never weaken them.

Audit trail

The trail follows the design fillslava built for ClaudeClaw: each row stores the previous hash and its own hash, so editing any row breaks verification from that row on; retention pruning moves a stored anchor so the remaining chain still verifies; secret-shaped values are refused at write time. Readers get scoped views: a compliance audience sees guardrail and STOP events, a deployer only the events of their own role, with e-mail and IP masked and every row still carrying its hash for independent checking.

06 · Infrastructure

P2P compute, credentials and the physical roadmap

Pulse relay over knitweb

Pulse is a trustless relay to compute with node-to-node sharing. Toddler reuses knitweb's proof-of-useful-work primitives: a peer's result is accepted only after sampled re-execution, and the sample size comes from knitweb's exact hypergeometric required_samples. For a job of 1,000 output blocks and a tolerated 1 % miss of a 1 % corruption, 368 blocks are re-run. With electricity at €0.30 per kWh and a local job of 36 MJ, local execution costs €3.00 while a peer at €1.00 plus verification costs €2.10; a peer whose collateral makes fraud profitable is refused, and peers above 90 % load are skipped. The woven knowledge graph (pull request #5) compiles to a deterministic knitweb synaptic bundle whose round trip is checked by that pull request's tests; until the operator signs it, it is written as an unsigned file that must not be shared.

Credentials and multiple identities

A toddler has several e-mail identities: one per provider, so a revoked key never exposes another account. Keys are stored in OpenBao under toddler/<identity>/<provider>/<key>. Creation uses only official endpoints (Google API Keys, Cloudflare tokens, OpenAI service accounts, GitHub App installation tokens, AWS IAM) behind the operator-approval STOP rule; Anthropic and TransIP do not allow key creation by API and become human tasks. Rotation never revokes the old key before the new one is verified. Mass account creation, CAPTCHA bypass and temp-mail farming are out of scope. This module is written and tested locally but is not in the repository; it is pushed only after explicit operator approval.

Physical roadmap

The physical Toddler combines camera and lidar perception, industrial-grade motion precision (ISO 10218, ISO/TS 15066 contact limits), learned policies in the style of current legged and humanoid platforms trained in simulation, and Jev answering many physical-rule questions in parallel for the reflex layer. The reflex is already implemented in software: a measured force, speed or proximity limit stops the robot whatever Jev reports, a late (> 20 ms) or missing answer stops it, and an uncertain answer counts at its upper bound. Real hardware waits for a care partner, an ethics committee and a CE/MDR path.

07 · Status

Results, limitations and status

What is delivered

  • On main: objective, STOP rules, structure, evaluation, relay, fast path and provenance, tested on real public data or explicit small inputs; CI runs the suite on every pull request.
  • In review (#2-#5, #7, #8): specialisation, audit trail, configuration and GUI, Jev client, knowledge base, resource governor and the v0.1 review fixes.
  • Knowledge base: 36 AI/NN concepts from 1796 to 2023 fetched with hash and licence; a woven graph linking concepts, the 25 study steps and code; its test asserts that every step resolves to code (pull request #5).
  • Configurator GUI with client-side validation mirrored and enforced in Python; tested end to end in a headless browser.

Limitations

  • The mapping from infant brains is an analogy; the study is correlational, single-cohort and needs replication, and so does every structural claim about Toddler.
  • Reward and utility weights are documented starting points, not calibrated values.
  • The structure module is validated on social-network data, not yet on Toddler's own versions; that needs many recorded versions first.
  • Competing with large robotics programmes on scale is not a goal; the contribution is a governed, auditable base for fine-tuning open models.
For developers
Start from toddler-config/v1: define the role, add your company's guardrails, and register experts only with measured evidence. Run python3 -m pytest -q before every deployment.

B. Glossary

Clustering coefficient - the share of a node's neighbours that are connected to each other (0 to 1).

Consensus clustering - repeating a non-deterministic clustering many times and clustering the co-assignment matrix.

Jev - a model that answers many typed questions in parallel and returns probabilities with uncertainty; used as a feature generator, never as the sole authority.

Pulse - knitweb's trustless relay to compute and node-to-node resource sharing.

STOP rule - a hard constraint that vetoes an action before utility is compared.
