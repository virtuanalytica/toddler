# G4: prospective mastered-parent fallback

The first two G4-search cohorts improved four of five `unlockpickup` routes. The fifth G3 parent, `t5001`, solved all 50 maps in both public sets and scored above the documented solved threshold on both. Its best new profile did not improve either set. Requiring a new expert for that already mastered child would discard four gains while adding no new capability.

Before creating any `G4-search` private seed set, the public gate now permits either five improved children or four improved children plus one **unchanged** G3 route. The unchanged parent's public development and check scores must each show 50/50 successes and normalised mean at least 1.0. The evaluator verifies that the fallback child's inherited expert weights and route match G3 exactly. This rule does not relax the private gate: two independent 30-seed trials must still pass the frozen ancestor and parent comparisons at `p < 0.01`, preserve mastered skills and satisfy the 0.75 improvement-probability requirement.

The 10 October cohorts are ineligible because their public sets were reused during development. The new one-family rule accepts only a cohort created after 10 October 20:00 UTC with a disjoint 50+50 public split not used by an earlier cohort. The future private seeds are generated only after that public preflight. The earlier failed private G4-oracle trial remains historical evidence and was not re-scored or retested to choose this public rule.

This opens a valid path to confirmation; it does not predict that the five-child statistical test has enough power. If the next eligible cohort fails, the family lock remains spent and G3 remains official. Further experiments need a newly registered family, a larger independent cohort or a preregistered matched-seed analysis, and fresh hidden sets.
